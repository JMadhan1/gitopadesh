Chapter 1: Arjuna Vishada Yoga

Arjuna's Dejection · 47 verses

Ask Krishna about this chapter
  • Verse 1

    Dhritarashtra said: Assembled on the holy field of Kurukshetra, eager for battle, what did my sons and the sons of Pandu do, O Sanjaya?

  • Verse 2

    Sanjaya said: Then, seeing the army of the Pandavas arrayed in battle formation, King Duryodhana approached his teacher and spoke these words.

  • Verse 3

    Behold, O teacher, this great army of the sons of Pandu, arrayed in battle formation by the son of Drupada, your intelligent disciple.

  • Verse 4

    Here are mighty warriors and great bowmen, equal in battle to Bhima and Arjuna — Yuyudhana, Virata, and Drupada, the great chariot-warrior.

  • Verse 5

    Dhrishtaketu, Chekitana, and the valiant king of Kashi, Purujit, Kuntibhoja, and Shaibya, the best among men.

  • Verse 6

    The valiant Yudhamanyu and the mighty Uttamaujas, the son of Subhadra and the sons of Draupadi — all of them indeed great chariot-warriors.

  • Verse 7

    But know also, O best among the twice-born, those who are distinguished among us, the leaders of my army; I name them to you for your information.

  • Verse 8

    You come first; then Bheeshma, Karna, Kripa, great warriors; Ashwaththama, Vikarna, and the son of Somadhatta;

  • Verse 9

    And many others, all ready to die for My sake; all armed, all skilled in warfare.

  • Verse 10

    Our army, marshalled under the protection of Bhishma, is unlimited, while this army of theirs, marshalled under the protection of Bhima, is limited.

  • Verse 11

    Therefore, all of you, stationed in your respective positions at every strategic point, must protect Bhishma alone, each and every one of you.

  • Verse 12

    Then, to enliven his spirits, the brave Grandfather Bheeshma, the eldest of the Kuru clan, blew his conch, its sound resembling that of a lion's roar.

  • Verse 13

    And immediately, all the conches, drums, trumpets, and horns blared forth in a tumultuous uproar.

  • Verse 14

    Then, seated in their spacious war chariot, yoked with white horses, Lord Shri Krishna and Arjuna sounded their divine conch shells.

  • Verse 15

    Lord Shri Krishna blew his Panchajanya, and Arjuna blew his Devadatta; brave Bheema blew his renowned shell, Poundra.

  • Verse 16

    The King Dharmaraja, the son of Kunti, blew the Anantavijaya, Nakula, and Sahadeva, the Sughosa and Manipushpaka, respectively.

  • Verse 17

    And the Maharaja of Benares, the great archer, Shikhandi, the great soldier, Dhrishtayumna, Virata, and Satyaki, the invincible one,

  • Verse 18

    Drupada and the sons of Draupadi, all of them, O ruler of the earth, and the mighty-armed son of Subhadra, blew their conch shells each separately.

  • Verse 19

    The tumult rent the hearts of the sons of Dhritarashtra, shaking heaven and earth with its echoing violence.

  • Verse 20

    Then, seeing the sons of Dhritarashtra arrayed in battle formation, as the clash of weapons was about to begin, the son of Pandu, whose banner bore a monkey, took up…

  • Verse 21

    Arjuna said: O Achyuta, place my chariot in the middle between the two armies, O lord of earth. Wait — let me reconsider the structure. "Mahīpate" is Sanjaya addressing…

  • Verse 22

    So that I may look upon these warriors arrayed here eager to fight, and know with whom I must do battle in this great combat.

  • Verse 23

    I wish to observe those who have assembled here, ready to fight, desiring to please the evil-minded son of Dhritarashtra in battle.

  • Verse 24

    Sanjaya said: "Having listened to Arjuna's request, Lord Shri Krishna drew up His bright chariot in the midst between the two armies.

  • Verse 25

    In the presence of Bhishma, Drona, and all the rulers of the earth, he said, "O Partha, behold these Kurus assembled together.

  • Verse 26

    There Partha saw stationed fathers, grandfathers, teachers, maternal uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, and friends as well.

  • Verse 27

    Fathers-in-law and benefactors were arrayed on both sides, and Arjuna then gazed at all those kinsmen before him.

  • Verse 28

    And his heart melted with pity, and sadly he spoke: "O my Lord! When I see all these, my own people, thirsting for battle,

  • Verse 29

    My limbs are failing me, my throat is parched, my body is trembling, and my hair is standing on end.

  • Verse 30

    The bow Gandiva slips from my hand, and my skin burns. I cannot remain still, for my mind is in tumult.

  • Verse 31

    I see only adverse omens, O Keshava, and I do not foresee any good from killing my own kinsmen in battle.

  • Verse 32

    Ah, my Lord! I do not crave victory, nor a kingdom, nor any pleasure. What would a kingdom, happiness, or life be to me?

  • Verse 33

    Those for whose sake we desire kingdom, enjoyments, and pleasures — they stand here arrayed in battle, having given up their lives and wealth.

  • Verse 34

    Teachers, fathers, grandfathers, sons, grandsons, uncles, fathers-in-law, brothers-in-law, and other relatives.

  • Verse 35

    I would not kill them, even for three worlds; why then for this earth alone? It matters not if I myself am killed.

  • Verse 36

    My Lord! What happiness can come from the death of these sons of Dhritarashtra? We shall be committing a sin if we kill these desperate men.

  • Verse 37

    Therefore, it is not right for us to kill the sons of Dhritarashtra, our own kinsmen; for how can we be happy after slaying our own people, O Madhava?

  • Verse 38

    Although these men, blinded by greed, see no guilt in destroying their kin or fighting against their friends,

  • Verse 39

    Should we not, whose eyes are open and who consider it wrong to annihilate our family, turn away from such a great crime?

  • Verse 40

    The destruction of our kindred means the destruction of the traditions of our ancient lineage, and when these are lost, irreligion will overrun our homes.

  • Verse 41

    O Krishna, when irreligion prevails, the women of the family become corrupt; and when women are corrupted, O Varshneya, there arises an intermixture of castes.

  • Verse 42

    Such intermixture of castes leads to hell both the destroyers of the family and the family itself; for their ancestors fall, deprived of the offerings of rice-balls and…

  • Verse 43

    By these sins of the destroyers of the family, which cause the intermixture of castes, the eternal duties of the community and the traditions of the family are ruined.

  • Verse 44

    O Janardana, we have heard that for those human beings whose family traditions have been destroyed, dwelling in hell for an indefinite period is certain.

  • Verse 45

    Alas, what a great sin we have resolved to commit, that out of greed for the pleasures of a kingdom we are prepared to slay our own kinsmen!

  • Verse 46

    If, on the contrary, the sons of Dhritarashtra, with weapons in hand, should slay me, unarmed and unresisting, surely that would be better for my welfare!

  • Verse 47

    Sanjaya said: "Having spoken thus, Arjuna sank into the seat of his chariot, discarding his bow and arrow, overwhelmed with grief in the midst of the armies."
