  • Verse 3

    Some philosophers say that all action is evil and should be abandoned, whereas others maintain that acts of sacrifice, benevolence, and austerity should not be given up.

  • Verse 4

    Hear from Me the firm conclusion regarding tyaga, O best of the Bharatas; for tyaga, O tiger among men, has been declared to be of three kinds.

  • Verse 5

    Acts of sacrifice, charity, and austerity should not be abandoned but must certainly be performed, for sacrifice, charity, and austerity are indeed purifiers of the wise.

  • Verse 6

    These actions too must certainly be performed, but with abandonment of attachment and desire for their fruits — this, O Partha, is my definite and supreme conviction.

  • Verse 7

    The renunciation of prescribed duty is not justified; abandoning it out of delusion is declared to be tamasic.

    Whatever action a person performs, whether by muscular effort, speech, or thought, and whether it be right or wrong, these five are the essential causes.

  • Verse 20

  • Verse 21

    The knowledge that perceives the manifold existence in all beings as separate comes from passion.

  • Verse 22

    But that which clings blindly to one idea as if it were all, without logic, truth, or insight, has its origin in darkness.

  • Verse 24

    But action that is performed by one desirous of fulfilling desires, or with egoism, and with much strain — that is declared to be rajasic.

  • Verse 25

    That action which is undertaken out of delusion, without regard for consequences, loss, injury, and one's own ability, is declared to be tamasic.

  • Verse 26

    The doer who is free from attachment, devoid of egoism, endowed with firmness and enthusiasm, and unaffected by success or failure, is said to be sattvic.

  • Verse 27

    The doer who is passionate, who craves the fruits of action, who is greedy, violent by nature, impure, and subject to joy and sorrow, is declared to be rajasic.

  • Verse 28

    While he whose purpose is weak, who is low-minded, stubborn, dishonest, malicious, lazy, despondent, and procrastinating—he may be assumed to be in darkness.

  • Verse 29

    Now hear the threefold division of intellect and also of firmness, according to the gunas, as it is being declared fully and distinctly, O Dhananjaya.

  • Verse 33

  • Verse 34

  • Verse 35
